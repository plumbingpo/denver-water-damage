Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Fix the source first, and only then patch or paint a water stain.
  • Rebuilt bathroom walls last longer when the exhaust fan runs through and after each shower.
  • Once a year, renew the caulk at sinks, showers and tubs to keep water from entering the wall.
  • Save spare tile and flooring, plus paint color details, for matching later repairs.
  • No wall or floor should be sealed by any contractor until you have seen final moisture readings.
  • When kitchens, bathrooms or laundry rooms are rebuilt, choose backer board and moisture resistant drywall.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Wall cavities remain open where drywall was removed for drying.
  • Interior doors now stick or will not latch after swelling from moisture.
  • Wood floor boards stayed raised, cupped or crowned even once the moisture was gone.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
  • Where water ran down a wall, the paint has blistered, peeled or turned brown.
  • Baseboards and door casings are warped or no longer sit flat against the wall.
  • Grout is breaking up and some tiles have lifted where moisture reached the floor below.

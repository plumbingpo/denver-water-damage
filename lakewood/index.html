Typical Water Damage Problems in Lakewood

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. The list below covers what we are asked to clean up most for Lakewood owners.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. The first sign is often a rusty puddle or a damp ring on the floor around the base. If the tank ruptures, it keeps refilling and draining onto the floor until someone closes the cold supply valve. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Leaks also start around chimneys, vent pipes and skylights where the sealant has dried out. Water travels along rafters before it drips, so the wet spot inside may be far from the hole outside. For roof leaks in Lakewood, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Depending on the season, a hard overnight freeze can burst lines that were fine the day before. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. A house left empty or a wing kept unheated faces the greatest risk. No flow or a bare trickle at the tap on a frigid day means a frozen line, so close the main and get help.

Backups: Gurgling sounds and more than one slow fixture at once suggest the main drain is partly blocked. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. The water is unsanitary, and hard surfaces need disinfecting after the solids and liquid are removed. Stay out of the affected rooms and avoid running water until the line is cleared.

Seepage: A wet line along the bottom of a foundation wall is a sign that water is building up in the soil next to it. Short downspouts, soil that slopes toward the house and blocked footing drains are frequent causes. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings. How Drying Works on a Lakewood Water Damage Job

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. On each Lakewood job, our task is to make that happen quickly and under control.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. That vapor is collected by dehumidifiers and pumped away, keeping the air dry so the process does not stall. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. Most homes in Lakewood reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

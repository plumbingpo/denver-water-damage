Drying Out a Colorado Springs Property: What to Expect

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. Carpet can be dry on top while the pad is soaked, and painted drywall can look fine while the back of it is wet. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. Our Colorado Springs technicians carry probe meters for wood and wallboard, scanning meters that read through finishes, and infrared cameras that pick up damp areas as cooler spots.

Different materials let go of water at different speeds. Drywall and carpet give up moisture fast, but studs, subflooring, hardwood and concrete are slow. The layout of dehumidifiers and air movers follows those rates and is changed as each day's numbers come in. A span of 3 to 5 days covers most jobs, and we will tell you early if yours looks like an exception.

The expensive error is pulling equipment too soon. Materials closed up while still damp can grow mold, swell, or come loose months later, long after the job seemed finished. So for every Colorado Springs job we dry to a measured target and keep the readings on file.

Preventing Water Damage in Colorado Springs Homes

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. Knowing the usual suspects is most of the prevention for a Colorado Springs homeowner.

Pipes and fittings top the list. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Next come appliances. Do not run the washing machine or dishwasher when nobody is home, and replace the hoses on a schedule instead of waiting for one to fail. The roof and the ground outside are third. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Leaving the house empty for a while? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. Homeowners in Colorado Springs can ask Denver Restoration Co about any of this. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.

Where Water Damage Starts in Colorado Springs Properties

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. Our crews in Colorado Springs follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in Colorado Springs, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: A clogged sewer line makes itself known at whichever drain sits lowest. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in Colorado Springs.
